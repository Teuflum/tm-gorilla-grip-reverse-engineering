02

How icing itself rises or fades

These lines show a single tire's icing percentage over time if it stays in one condition. This is a different quantity from graph 1's force weight.

Idealized fixed conditions at driving speed above the tested model's threshold: Ice/RoadIce builds from 0 to 100% in 1,650 ms; grounded non-ice such as plastic loses 100 percentage points in 3,300 ms; air loses them in 6,000 ms. During decay the minimum is the smaller of starting icing and wetness at the transition. This graph holds wetness fixed; in play, wetness and contact can change.

03

Does right mode get stored before takeoff?

Example: switch raw input from full left to full right. The internal steering moves gradually. Drag the deadline to the last physics update with at least one wheel touching.

The right-mode test is strictly greater than +10%, on a physics update with any wheel in contact. This is a normalized internal steering value, not VehicleState's FLSteerAngle or raw InputSteer. If the threshold is not crossed before takeoff, the prior left mode stays. The 0.2 change per update was measured in the tested ice conditions; six affected updates means roughly 50–60 ms after the input switch, depending on update alignment. The first wheel to lift does not end the opportunity; the last wheel does.

04

A direction threshold is not a full-force threshold

The ±10% steering line decides which direction is stored. The recovered tire-force multiplier also depends on how far internal steering is turned.

In the measured high-speed ice samples the target is about 1 + |internal steering|^1.5: 0% → 1.0, 20% → 1.089, 60% → 1.465, 100% → 2.0. The model also depends on speed. On the clean tarmac start of ANGULAR ↻ MOMENTUM, this icy multiplier stayed at its baseline 1.0. This multiplier acts on a tire-force term, not on vehicle speed. Steering near zero can temporarily reduce it even while the stored direction survives the 300 ms neutral timeout.

05

Why setting the direction before a jump feels instant

Changing stored direction resets the tire-force multiplier to 1.0. The measured delayed landing stayed low for about 400 ms and reached 2.0 at about 600 ms. Airtime can use up this wait.

The blue curve is an illustrative interpolation through approximate landmarks from one measured full-steering landing (near 1.0 through 370 ms, about 1.05 at 390 ms, 2.0 near 590 ms). It is not the exact game recovery formula. The green line shifts that same sketch earlier by the selected lead time. Full steering and similar high-speed conditions are assumed. Neutral steering for about 300 ms is a separate stored-direction timeout, not this force-recovery time.

06

What the 300 ms neutral timer remembers

Start with left mode stored, then let internal steering stay between −10% and +10% while a wheel touches. Drag how long it stays there before returning left.

The graph shows stored direction, not the current force multiplier. Near-zero steering can lower the multiplier even before the stored mode clears. The 300 ms line is the timer threshold; the mode clears on the next eligible physics update at or after it. This simplified graph assumes continuous wheel contact and an active icy tire-force branch. In the air the neutral check does not run; at landing the timer may be checked if steering is still neutral.
